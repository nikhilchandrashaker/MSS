01

What this is

This document explains the research model behind the companion spreadsheet, which projects each Mario Super Sluggers character onto a real baseball defensive position — and, new in this pass, tags each one with a real-world MLB style comp. The goal was never to just report a character's highest stat and call it a day. It's to ask what position a player with that statistical profile would actually fit on a real diamond, the way scouts think about tools and the defensive spectrum, not "biggest number wins."

03

Raw columns & derived indices

The spreadsheet's Roster Data sheet carries each character's Team, Character Family (the base species/character a color-swap or baby variant belongs to), Color Variant flag, Captain flag, Throwing/Batting Arm, and the four native 1–10 ratings, transcribed directly from the wiki. Four derived indices sit alongside them:

IndexFormulaWhy
Overall TotalPitch+Bat+Field+RunBlunt power ranking, not used in the position model.
Offensive Index0.75·Bat + 0.25·RunSpeed adds real offensive value — extra bases, steals.
Defensive Index0.70·Field + 0.30·RunRange depends on hands/instincts and raw speed.
Athletic Indexavg(Pitch, Field, Run)A rough "tool grade" excluding hitting.

Why Bat needs a caveat. MSS's Bat rating is a single combined offensive number (power and contact folded together), unlike real scouting, which grades Hit and Power separately. Two characters with an identical Bat score could translate very differently in real life — a slap hitter versus a three-outcome slugger — and this dataset can't tell them apart. That's a genuine limitation of the translation, not something the model below tries to paper over.

04

The position model

Each character gets a 0–10 fit score at all nine positions, weighted to mirror the real defensive spectrum: catcher/shortstop/second are defense-heavy and bat-optional, first/left field are bat-heavy and defense-optional, center sits in between but leans on range and speed. "Field" stands in for both glove and arm, since MSS doesn't split them — an approximation, not a perfect one. Every weight vector below was also checked so that it's mathematically possible for some stat profile to make it the single best fit — an early draft had Second Base as a pure blend of Third and Shortstop, which made it structurally unable to ever win.

MLB names here are style comps for the statistical archetype only — a shorthand for "plays like," not a claim about the real player.

The throwing-arm rule. In real baseball, left-handed throwers essentially never play Catcher, Second, Third, or Shortstop — those throws are built around a right-handed release. The model enforces this: a Left-throwing character's C/2B/3B/SS scores are disqualified when picking an Optimal Position, even if the raw formula score is high. It's why lefty throwers like Waluigi, Bowser Jr., Boo, Funky Kong, King K. Rool, and the whole Dry Bones family land at Pitcher, First Base, or an outfield corner in the model, even when their Field rating alone looks like a middle infielder.

05

Model vs. the naive read

"Optimal Position (Model)" is the eligible position with the highest weighted score. "Naive Stat-Based Role" just flags whichever single raw stat is highest — no weighting, no handedness rule, the "basic spreadsheet" approach kept alongside it for comparison. The two are meant to disagree sometimes; that disagreement is the interesting part of this project, not a bug.

Mario Pitch 6 · Bat 7 · Field 6 · Run 7 → Naive: Bat-first (corner/1B) → Model: Left Field

An empirical finding worth flagging. Even with every position mathematically reachable, only 8 of 9 actually get claimed across these 72 characters — nobody projects as a true Third Baseman, and Right Field is thin too. MSS's cast skews toward pure sluggers, pure burners, and pure defenders/pitchers; very few combine strong Bat and strong Field without a big Run number, which is exactly the profile a hot-corner third baseman needs. Worth digging into further.

06

Limitations

  • Four game stats can't fully capture real baseball tools — no separate arm rating, no Hit/Power split, no pitch-type or velocity data, no plate discipline.
  • Position weights are this project's own heuristic, built to mirror the real defensive spectrum — a reasonable model, not a validated scouting formula.
  • "Field" stands in for both glove and arm strength; a character rated a poor overall fielder could still plausibly have played an arm-driven position like RF in reality.
  • The Mii's Throwing/Batting Arm varies by chosen gender in-game; the dataset defaults to the right-handed (male) animation for modeling purposes.
  • "Character Family" groupings are a judgment call — e.g. weapon-variant Bros (Hammer/Fire/Boomerang) are grouped like color variants even though they differ by more than palette.
  • MLB comps are stylistic tags describing a statistical archetype, generated from simple threshold rules on the same four stats — they are a fun translation layer, not a scouting-grade player match.
